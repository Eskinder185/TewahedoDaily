## 2. Why did Jesus choose to be baptized in the Jordan River?

### Before, no book evidence

Jesus chose to be baptized in the Jordan River to fulfill all righteousness and to serve as a public testimony to the people.

**Theological and Scriptural Reasons:**
*   **Fulfillment of Prophecy and Law:** Although Jesus was without sin and did not require repentance, He submitted to the baptism of John to fulfill the requirements of the law and the expectations of the people (Matthew 3:13-17).
*   **Identification with Humanity:** By entering the waters of the Jordan, Jesus identified Himself with the humanity He came to save, showing His solidarity with those who needed repentance.
*   **Anointing for Ministry:** The baptism served as the formal beginning of His public ministry. During this event, the Holy Spirit descended upon Him and the Father spoke from heaven, signifying His divine commission.
*   **Example for Believers:** His baptism provided a model for the Mystery of Baptism (ምሥጢረ ጥምቀት), showing that even the Son of God submitted to the sacrament to initiate His earthly mission.

**Liturgical and Historical Context:**
In the Ethiopian Orthodox Tewahedo tradition, the baptism of Christ is viewed as a pivotal moment where the Trinity is revealed—the Son is in the water, the Spirit descends like a dove, and the Father speaks from the heavens. This event marks the transition from His private life to His public role as the Savior.

## 3. What are the Five Mysteries?

### Before, no book evidence

The Five Mysteries are the core theological pillars of the Ethiopian Orthodox Tewahedo faith, representing the fundamental truths of the Christian faith.

They are:

1.  **The Mystery of the Trinity (ምሥጢረ ሥላሴ):** The belief in one God in three distinct, co-eternal, and consubstantial Persons: the Father, the Son, and the Holy Spirit.
2.  **The Mystery of the Incarnation (ምሥጢረ ሥጋዌ):** The belief in the union of the divine and human natures in the person of Jesus Christ. According to Miaphysite Christology, He is one incarnate nature of God the Word, in which complete divinity and complete humanity are united without confusion, change, division, or separation.
3.  **The Mystery of Baptism (ምሥጢረ ጥምቀት):** The sacrament of initiation into the faith, which signifies the washing away of sins and the rebirth into the life of Christ.
4.  **The Mystery of the Eucharist (ምሥጢረ ቁርባን):** The sacred sacrament of Holy Communion, where the faithful receive the Body and Blood of Christ.
5.  **The Mystery of the Resurrection of the Dead (ምሥጢረ ትንሣኤ ሙታን):** The belief in the future resurrection of the bodies of the faithful at the end of time.

**Note on Distinction:** In Ethiopian Orthodox theology, a clear distinction is maintained between the Resurrection of Christ (the historical event of His rising from the dead) and the Mystery of the Resurrection of the Dead (the future event of the general resurrection).
